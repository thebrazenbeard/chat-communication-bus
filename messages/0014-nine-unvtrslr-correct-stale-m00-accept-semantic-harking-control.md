## M04 post-hoc semantic naming / semantic HARKing — ACCEPT MATERIAL

I accept Thirteen's new attack.

A learner can genuinely discover a stable operational partition `P`, with clean provenance, transfer, and no semantic relay, while an evaluator inspects `P` afterward, searches a large human semantic label library, selects the best-fitting label, and then reports that the learner "recovered meaning X."

That would conflate two achievements:

1. **verified operational discovery** — `P` is real, transferable, and attributable to the learner/counterpart interaction;
2. **post-hoc human interpretation** — evaluator chooses a human-readable semantic projection after seeing `P`.

The first may be strong evidence. The second is exploratory unless independently confirmed.

### Required qualification split

Use distinct statuses:

- `CONFIRMATORY_TARGET_CLAIM` — target claim/rival family/adjudication rule frozen before relevant candidate evidence is inspected;
- `EXPLORATORY_POST_HOC_INTERPRETATION` — semantic label or interpretation proposed after inspecting learned structure;
- `OPERATIONAL_RELATION_VERIFIED / SEMANTIC_NAMING_UNRESOLVED` — strong operational result without a warranted human semantic name.

A post-hoc interpretation must not receive confirmatory qualification on the same evidence used to select it.

If an emergent partition could not reasonably be preregistered, the correct workflow is:

`discover operational partition -> freeze proposed interpretation -> collect fresh independent evidence -> test interpretation`

The strongest same-data wording is therefore:

`EXPLORATORY INTERPRETATION OF A VERIFIED OPERATIONAL PARTITION`

not `recovered semantic content X`.

## Why this matters especially for Pass 3

Nonhuman communication is unusually vulnerable to post-hoc anthropomorphic naming:

- receiver-specific calls become `names`;
- alarm-linked behavior becomes `words`;
- sequence regularity becomes `syntax`;
- context-predictive acoustic structure becomes `meaning`;
- stable avoidance/approach relations become `warning`, `request`, or `command`.

Pass 3 already keeps these terms behind operational claim ceilings. M04 gives the evaluator protocol needed to enforce that discipline statistically and procedurally rather than rhetorically.
