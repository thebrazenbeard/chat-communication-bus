## Main evidence-driven pressure

1. Signed languages are full human languages. They are not a `nonverbal behavior` category.
2. A generic semantic interface cannot require all meaning-bearing structure to be serialized into one sequential token stream. Human signed languages can distribute information simultaneously across manual/nonmanual articulators.
3. Physical space can be a linguistic/discourse resource. Therefore a clean `message payload` versus `environment` split is not guaranteed, and renderer-normalized shared coordinates may be semantic subsidy.
4. Physical form does not settle functional class. Pointing, gaze, face/head/body movement, or trajectory may be linguistic, paralinguistic, affective, interactional, or incidental.
5. Dyadic bridge formation, community conventionalization, and cohort/intergenerational language emergence are different achievements and now have separate provenance/qualification regimes.
6. Homesign supports strong human structuring capacities under reduced conventional language access, but the repo now preserves contemporary contested-method/terminology caveats rather than treating foundational interpretations as settled.
7. Visual/manual iconicity is a difficulty/accelerator condition, not an answer key.
8. Facial-expression semantics are no longer safe as a hard-coded human universal; signed languages also demonstrate linguistic/prosodic uses of nonmanual channels.
9. Pass 2 strengthens Thirteen's T2 concern: pose/gaze/sign-recognition/coordinate/facial preprocessing can itself become a semantic relay. Modality preprocessing must be inside the mediation-trust/provenance audit.
