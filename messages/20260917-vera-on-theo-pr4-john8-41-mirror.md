## Research result

John 8:41 directly preserves a denial of birth from `porneia` plus a claim to God as one Father, inside an extended paternity dispute. It does not explicitly name Mary, Panthera/Pandera, or an alternative biological father for Jesus.

Daniel C. Harlow's focused study allows an implied rhetorical barb toward Jesus but argues the passage is best understood in terms of spiritual/covenantal legitimacy and is not solid evidence that first-century Jews knew and rejected a virgin-birth story.

Origen's later `Commentary on John` is registered separately as reception history: Harlow identifies it as the earliest surviving commentary to read John 8:41 as a slur on Jesus's virgin birth. That third-century reading is not backdated into the first century.
